# European Defence Tech Hackathon

> Note on spelling. The brand uses "European Defense Tech Hackathon" with American spelling throughout. The British "Defence" is used in narrative here; quoted event names and URLs keep the brand spelling.

The European Defence Tech Hackathon is the flagship event series of the [[CRM/networks/European Defence Tech Hub/European Defence Tech Hub|European Defence Tech Hub]] (EDTH). It is a recurring three-day weekend hackathon, replicated across European cities at a roughly monthly cadence and supplemented with regional Defence Tech Weeks, specialist editions, and online demo days.

The format is consistent. Hackers arrive on Friday around midday, work over Saturday and Sunday in self-organising teams against publicly-posted challenges, and pitch on Sunday afternoon to a jury of operators, founders, investors, and partner organisations. Mentors circulate through the venue all weekend.

External: [eurodefense.tech](https://eurodefense.tech); calendar at [lu.ma/eurodefensetech](https://lu.ma/eurodefensetech).

## What's typically on

- **Friday opening and challenge briefing.** Sponsoring partners and operator partners (e.g. Snake Island Institute, BRAVE1, TYTAN Technologies, Fire Point, Quantum Systems) post real challenges drawn from current battlefield or procurement reality.
- **Saturday build day.** Teams form around challenges, often crossing nationalities and disciplines (engineering, defence policy, operations, design). Mentors and partner technologists drop in throughout the day.
- **Saturday side sessions.** Workshops, partner sessions (e.g. counter-UAS workshops, pitch coaching, country-specific procurement primers), and "Women in Defence Tech" or similar community breakfasts.
- **Sunday morning final build.** Teams race to ship a working prototype or demo.
- **Sunday Demo Day.** Three-minute pitches plus two-minute Q&A in front of a curated jury. Prizes and special-partner awards are announced at the close.
- **Post-event.** Submissions and outcomes are summarised on EDTH's LinkedIn and in the newsletter, with named team and individual call-outs that feed founder-formation in the months following.

## Why it matters for defence-sector SMEs

- **Founder pipeline.** Many of the defence-tech companies coming out of European universities and engineering programmes between 2024 and 2026 trace their origin to an EDTH hackathon weekend. The Hub explicitly optimises for the hacker-to-founder loop.
- **Operator feedback in 48 hours.** Hackathons fuse founders with active or recently-serving operators. The feedback loop is faster and more honest than most procurement channels.
- **Discovery surface for partners.** Demo Day juries are stacked with investors, primes, and government users. A working prototype in front of the right jury is a credible introduction to that audience.
- **Community surface beyond the event.** Hackathon graduates carry into the EDTH community subscription, the Discord, the New Defense Post newsletter, and subsequent webinars.

## How to engage

- **Hack.** Apply through the relevant city's Luma listing as a hacker. No defence background required; engineering and design talent is the bar.
- **Mentor or sit on the jury.** Invitation-only. Surfacing into the mentor pool tends to flow through an existing mentor, an EDTH team member, or a partner organisation.
- **Sponsor or co-host.** Partner conversations sit alongside the hackathon programme rather than a separate sales surface. EDTH co-hosts with venues (EY, Marine Technology Center of Sweden, universities) and challenge sponsors (Snake Island Institute, BRAVE1, TYTAN, Fire Point, Quantum Systems).
- **Attend the planned New Defence Summit.** EDTH's announced summit format consolidates the wider programme into a single flagship event.
