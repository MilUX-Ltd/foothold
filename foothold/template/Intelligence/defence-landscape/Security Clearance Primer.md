# Security Clearance Primer

Ten minutes of orientation before your first cleared conversation. Everything here comes from published UKSV and MOD guidance; where this page and gov.uk disagree, gov.uk wins. This is not folklore and it is not advice on your individual case.

## The levels that matter

- **BPSS** (Baseline Personnel Security Standard). Not a clearance; an identity and background check. The floor for most government-adjacent work.
- **CTC** (Counter Terrorist Check). Proximity to public figures or sites vulnerable to terrorist attack.
- **SC** (Security Check). The workhorse. Regular, uncontrolled access to SECRET assets and occasional supervised access to TOP SECRET. Most defence-supplier roles that need "clearance" mean SC.
- **eSC** (enhanced SC). SC plus additional checks for specific roles.
- **DV** (Developed Vetting). Frequent, uncontrolled access to TOP SECRET. Deep-dive vetting including interview.
- **eDV** (enhanced DV). The highest level, specific sensitive roles.

Full definitions: [UKSV clearance levels](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/united-kingdom-security-vetting-clearance-levels/national-security-vetting-clearance-levels).

## Person versus company

Personnel clearance clears an individual. **Facility Security Clearance (FSC)** clears the company: needed when your organisation will hold or process classified material at your own site, rather than only supplying cleared people who work at the customer's. The [MOD FSC policy and guidance](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65f1c6789812278a47f613a6/20240313-MOD_Facility_Security_Clearance_Policy_and_Guidance_v1.4.pdf) is public and canonical. Many SMEs never need FSC; know which side of that line your delivery model sits on before you bid.

## Realistic timescales (2026)

Published targets and lived reality differ, and the gap is structural: UKSV's transformation programme was cancelled, so backlogs persist.

- **SC**: commonly one to three months, frequently longer. The original 25-working-day target is missed in most cases.
- **DV**: six to nine months.
- **Renewals**: SC is reviewed at ten years (seven for non-FSC contractors); DV at seven.

The practical consequence: a cleared hire starts in weeks, an uncleared one waits months. Contracts requiring cleared people are won by companies that already have them.

## What founders most often get wrong

1. **You cannot self-sponsor.** Clearance is sponsored by a contract or a sponsoring organisation (a customer, a prime, or an MOD body). "We'll just get everyone SC cleared" is not a unilateral option; win work that sponsors it, or team with a partner who can.
2. **Clearance follows the person, sponsorship follows the role.** A lapsed or unsponsored clearance can sometimes be reactivated quickly, but do not assume; verify status through the sponsor.
3. **Nationality and residency rules bite early.** UKSV checks typically need a UK footprint (residency history) to complete; long overseas periods slow or complicate vetting. Factor this into hiring for cleared roles.
4. **Put clearance in your bid qualification.** If delivery needs SC bodies on site in month one and you have none, that tender is not winnable this cycle. The `eligibility-check` skill in this pack asks this question for you.

## Watch: changes under way (October 2026)

Two changes could affect how vetting works and how long it takes. Neither has changed the process yet.

- **A government review of national security vetting.** Announced to Parliament on 20 April 2026, with terms of reference published by the Cabinet Office on 1 June 2026. It is a short review of the lessons for the national security vetting system. No findings have been published. Source: gov.uk [Vetting Review Terms of Reference](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vetting-review-terms-of-reference).
- **UKSV's home is moving.** The July 2026 machinery of government changes set up a new **OneGov Delivery Agency** in the Cabinet Office family to run operational services, including Security Vetting. For now UKSV is still listed as part of the Cabinet Office, and no move date has been given. Sources: gov.uk [Machinery of government changes fact sheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/machinery-of-government-changes-fact-sheet); [United Kingdom Security Vetting](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/united-kingdom-security-vetting).

Re-check timescales and contact routes with your sponsor before relying on anything above.

## What fails vetting

Only what published UKSV guidance says: vetting assesses honesty, integrity, financial vulnerability, and susceptibility to pressure. Undisclosed problems are worse than disclosed ones; the process is designed around candour. Anything beyond that published position is rumour, so treat war stories accordingly.
