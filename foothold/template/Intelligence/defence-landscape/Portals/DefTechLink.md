# DefTechLink

## Summary

DefTechLink is a paid private platform that aggregates defence and dual-use opportunities across roughly 29 allied countries into a single filterable feed. Unlike the UK government portals on this index (Helios, Contracts Finder, Defence Sourcing Portal, MOD Acquisition Pipeline, Central Digital Platform), DefTechLink is a commercial subscription service and goes multi-national rather than UK-only. It also pairs the opportunity feed with explainer content on procurement routes, contract types, and RDT&E funding for new entrants.

External: [deftechlink.com](https://deftechlink.com) (gated; public surface is a Subscription Required / Choose a Plan page). Public company description on the [DefTechLink LinkedIn page](https://www.linkedin.com/company/deftechlink/).

## Who it's for

DefTechLink positions itself for companies entering defence and dual-use markets, especially those expanding beyond their home country. The LinkedIn description (publicly visible) frames the value as procurement-pathway literacy rather than just listings: "Entering defence is rarely limited by technology. It is shaped by understanding users, procurement pathways, funding routes, timelines, and how different national systems actually work."

Most plausibly useful to:

- Defence-tech SMEs scoping international expansion (US DoD SBIR, NATO calls, Five Eyes adjacent procurement).
- Dual-use companies discovering how their commercial product maps onto defence procurement routes.
- Smaller teams without a dedicated bid-research function who need a single feed rather than monitoring multiple national portals.
- Founders who want plain-language explainers on the structural difference between markets (UK vs US vs European procurement, RDT&E vs production contracting).

Less obviously useful to companies already deep in UK defence procurement where the official UK portals plus an existing network already cover the field.

## What it offers

Public-source description from the LinkedIn company page and search-result excerpts of the platform's own marketing copy:

- **Opportunity database.** Filterable by geography, capability area, and source. Coverage described publicly as around 29 countries.
- **Events diary.** Listed as around 149 upcoming defence and dual-use events at signup (UK and international).
- **DefTechLearn.** Article library covering procurement basics, RDT&E funding, contract types, transition pathways.
- **DefTechPod.** Podcast and webinar series on how defence ecosystems work.
- **Trial.** One-month free trial available on name and email only.

The site itself is gated; the only pages visible without a subscription are an access-denied screen and 404s for unauthenticated routes.

## Pricing

DefTechLink charges **$35/month** after the one-month free trial. The pricing is not on the public-facing landing page (which only shows the access-denied screen), so this figure is sourced from the platform's own subscription messaging, including the `notifications@deftechlink.com` sender address used for billing correspondence. Confirm against current pricing at signup; the platform may revise this without updating external mentions.

## How DefTechLink differs from the UK government portals

|  | DefTechLink | UK gov portals (Helios, Contracts Finder, DSP, MOD Acq. Pipeline, CDP) |
|---|---|---|
| **Operator** | Private US-based company | UK Government / MOD / Hellios |
| **Cost** | Paid subscription (~$35/month) | Free |
| **Geography** | ~29 allied countries | UK only |
| **Format** | Aggregated feed + explainer content | Direct portal access |
| **Eligibility** | Open registration | UK SMEs / public-sector suppliers |

The two are complementary rather than substitutable. The UK government portals are the source of truth for UK MOD procurement; DefTechLink is a discovery layer over the wider allied opportunity space.

## How to evaluate the trial

Honest framing for a founder weighing the spend after the free month:

- If you sell consultancy or services through relationships and you're not bid-chasing, the cost is hard to justify on its own.
- If you're scoping international expansion, the international feed becomes a real time-saver and the price is cheap.
- If you want competitive-intelligence signal across allied markets (what categories of work are being posted where), the feed is one of the few single-stop options.
- The explainer content is useful early, less useful once you've found your route. Read what you can during the trial.
