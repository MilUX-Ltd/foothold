# Single-Source Contract Rules

When the MOD places a contract without competition, the **Single Source Contract Regulations 2014** set how the price and the supplier's profit are worked out, and what the supplier must report. The **Single Source Regulations Office (SSRO)** advises on them. In 2026 the MOD began reforming them to tie profit to delivery. This page says what has changed in law, what is promised but not yet law, and why a founder should care.

## Why a founder should care

- **Most SMEs are not caught today, and fewer will be.** The rules bite on qualifying single-source contracts above a value threshold. The MOD plans to raise that threshold from **£5m to £25m**, which it says would take nearly all SMEs out of the mandatory reporting rules while keeping 97% of single-source contract value inside the regime.
- **If you sell sole-source to a prime**, the prime's own contract may be regulated, and the incentive terms below change how it is paid and what it asks of its supply chain.
- **If you are about to win a large sole-source contract**, the profit you can earn now depends more on hitting agreed performance targets.

## In law now

**The Single Source Contract (Amendment) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/510)**: made 11 May 2026, laid 14 May 2026, **in force 5 June 2026**.

- Raises the permissible **incentive adjustment** on a qualifying defence contract from **2 to 10 percentage points**. The MOD's summary: maximum incentive payments rise from 2% to 10% of costs, paid only when agreed performance targets are met.
- The MOD must set an objectively verifiable outcome before the adjustment is payable; any judgement-based element is decided by a suitably qualified independent person.
- The SSRO's opinion remit is extended to cover the incentive adjustment.
- For **qualifying sub-contracts** the incentive adjustment **stays at 2 percentage points**.

## Announced, not yet law

The MOD's 14 May 2026 announcement promised a second statutory instrument, due before the summer recess, to:

- reduce profit floors on lower-risk contracts;
- add an **Innovation Uplift** rewarding firms that fund new products themselves without a guaranteed government contract, aimed particularly at smaller businesses and new entrants;
- raise the **threshold** at which contracts fall under the regulations **from £5m to £25m**.

We could not find this second instrument on legislation.gov.uk on 15 October 2026. Treat these three changes as planned, not in force, and check legislation.gov.uk before relying on them.
