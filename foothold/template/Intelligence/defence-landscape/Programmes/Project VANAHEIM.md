# Project VANAHEIM

## Summary

VANAHEIM is a joint UK-US counter-drone initiative, led by the British Army's RAPSTONE Task Force. The MOD describes it as "the first wave of a substantial programme of work updating the Army's C-UAS capabilities against the rapid development of Small UAS in recent years". It targets detection, tracking, identification and defeat of Class 1 UAS threats, specifically Class 1b/c including first-person view drones, drawing directly on lessons from Ukraine.

Rather than a conventional procurement competition, VANAHEIM runs experiments that place counter-drone technology directly with light infantry units, so performance is judged by generalist soldiers in realistic conditions. Partners include the Army Trials Unit, the Joint Effects Trials and Development Group, Soldierworks and US counterparts, with 13 industry suppliers involved. Test events ran in Germany and Poland in summer 2025; further trials follow in Lithuania in summer 2026 under the US-led Project FLYTRAP, with selected systems heading to Exercise Convergence Capstone in the US and a limited acquisition expected.

## Why it matters to suppliers

- **The entry bar is defined and public.** Solutions had to be usable by generalist soldiers, portable on foot or by light vehicle, GVA/GSA and SAPIENT-compliant, able to integrate with Battlespace Management Applications, and at TRL 6-9.
- **Experimentation is the route in.** This is a hands-on trials pathway where soldier feedback shapes selection, not a paper competition. If you build C-UAS or adjacent sensing/effects capability, the RAPSTONE experimentation cycle is a live route to putting kit in front of end users.
- **It signals the Army's direction of travel** on counter-UAS acquisition: iterative trials, allied interoperability, and rapid downselect rather than long single-award programmes.
