# Project CORVUS

## Summary

Project CORVUS is the British Army's programme for long-range surveillance and reconnaissance drones to **replace Watchkeeper**, which has been in service since 2014 and is nearing the end of its life.

On 25 July 2026 the MOD announced that **Tekever** would supply **AR5** long-endurance surveillance drones under a contract worth **up to £400m over ten years**. The initial order is six AR5s, with up to 24 delivered by 2029. Each can carry up to 50kg of cameras and sensors. The drones will be built at Tekever's new Swindon facility. Subject to contract signature and final approvals, the capability is due to enter service later in 2026, with support for at least five years and options to extend.

## Funding (Defence Investment Plan 2026)

The [[Intelligence/defence-landscape/Defence Investment Plan 2026|Defence Investment Plan 2026]] lists Project CORVUS at **£450m** in its land spending table for FY26/27 to FY29/30, of which **£310m** is described as new or additional investment, including the next generation of uncrewed aerial systems to replace Watchkeeper.

## Where a founder fits

The aircraft is awarded, so the openings are around it: sensors and payloads, ground control, data exploitation and the links into [[Intelligence/defence-landscape/Programmes/ASGARD|ASGARD]] and the [[Intelligence/defence-landscape/Programmes/Digital Targeting Web|Digital Targeting Web]], and supply-chain work with the prime.

Separately, in September 2026 the Army bought more than 1,000 small drones (£16m, from three UK suppliers) under Project RAPSTONE, its programme for getting new technology to soldiers faster. That is not part of CORVUS, but it shows small UK firms winning Army drone orders.
