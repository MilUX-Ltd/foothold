# Project ANVIL

## Summary

ANVIL is described by Dstl as "the first soldier-led development of Computer Vision Models to help drones find and identify targets, combining flexibility with rapid and accurate target identification". Computer vision lets the drone interpret imagery using AI and machine learning; the soldier-led loop means models can identify new targets and adapt to changing conditions within 24 hours.

ANVIL operates alongside the FALLING INSTRUMENTS Ground Control Station (FIGS) within an Initial Experimentation Systems (IES) framework. The IES is deployed with three Army units to build battlefield familiarity with computer vision, and is positioned as the foundational technology for a UK swarming UAS capability. The technical lineage runs through AUKUS Resilient Autonomy and AI Technologies (RAAIT), developed by Dstl and matured from concept into a deployable system.

## Why it matters to suppliers

- **Soldier-led model development is the new pattern.** End users retraining AI models in the field on a 24-hour cycle changes what "requirements" mean; capability evolves in service rather than being specified up front. Products and services that support this loop (data curation, model evaluation, operator tooling, training) have a natural home here.
- **AUKUS Pillar 2 made concrete.** ANVIL shows RAAIT output reaching deployed units, a signal that AUKUS AI and autonomy work is a real route to fielded capability, not just trilateral trials. See [[Intelligence/defence-landscape/Programmes/AUKUS Pillar 2|AUKUS Pillar 2]].
- **Upstream of swarming.** The IES is explicitly framed as the foundation for a UK swarming UAS capability, so ANVIL sits ahead of a significant future programme space.
