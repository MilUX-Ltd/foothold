# Defence Investment Plan 2026

The Defence Investment Plan (DIP) is the four-year spending plan that turns the 2025 Strategic Defence Review into budget lines. For a founder it is the best public demand signal there is: it says where the money goes, what was cancelled, and which new programmes will be buying. This page reads it that way. Figures are from the plan itself; where this page and the plan disagree, the plan wins.

## The plan in brief

- **Published:** 30 June 2026 by the Ministry of Defence and HM Treasury. [gov.uk publication page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-defence-investment-plan), with the [full plan (PDF, 81 pages)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a44e989167a99cf0018da38/The_Defence_Investment_Plan.pdf), a four-page summary and a funding explainer.
- **Money:** £298bn over the four financial years FY26/27 to FY29/30 (£297.7bn in Table 1), backed by a £15bn increase in the MOD's spending power above the 2025 Spending Review.
- **Scope:** a summary of the major investment choices, not the whole defence budget. Sensitive capabilities are left out.
- **Beyond 2030:** spending from 2030 to 2035 is deliberately left largely unallocated, so the MOD can buy technology that does not exist yet.
- **Spending commitments:** 3.5% of GDP on defence by 2035, with NATO allies; 3% of GDP "in the next Parliament", with plans to be set at the next spending review. NATO-qualifying spending is projected at 2.7% of GDP in 2027/28.
- **Novel technology:** at least 10% of the annual Equipment Programme budget goes on novel technology (AI, autonomy, quantum, directed energy). The plan shows 12% in FY26/27, rising to 16% (£3.2bn) in FY29/30.
- **Accountability:** the MOD will report progress to Parliament every year before the summer recess. The first delivery update is due by July 2027.

## Read it as an SME demand signal

Where the plan puts new money, new buyers follow. Four things stand out for small firms.

1. **Uncrewed systems and drones are the biggest new market.** Over £5bn on autonomous systems by 2030, spread across many programmes rather than one prime contract.
2. **AI and software have named budgets and front doors.** Taskforce RAID, Project FRONTIER, the Digital Targeting Web and UK Defence Innovation all have named budgets, and several take ideas directly from industry. The plan ring-fences Taskforce RAID's and UKDI's budgets; Project FRONTIER sits within a protected AI Investment Fund.
3. **Innovation money is protected.** UKDI's £400m a year is ring-fenced, and the novel-technology floor of 10% of the Equipment Programme is a commitment, not an aspiration.
4. **SME spend has a target.** The MOD will spend an additional £2.5bn with SMEs by 2028, a 50% increase in direct SME spend, led by the [[CRM/organisations/government/Defence Office for Small Business Growth|Defence Office for Small Business Growth]].

## Cancellations and changes

These are the decisions most likely to have made a page, a bid plan or a pitch out of date.

- **Multi-Role Strike Ship (MRSS): stopped.** The plan says MRSS was too complex for the Commando Force now planned. The UK is pivoting to explore the Netherlands-led Amphibious Transport Ship programme, with £240m for amphibious shipping in this period (2.4). See [[Intelligence/defence-landscape/Programmes/Multi-Role Strike Ship (MRSS)|Multi-Role Strike Ship (MRSS)]].
- **Type 83 destroyer and Type 32 frigate concepts: replaced** by the Hybrid Navy of crewed and uncrewed vessels. A new Hybrid Navy maritime air defence system, with a Common Combat Vessel as its "central brain", will eventually replace the Type 45 destroyers, due to retire from 2035 (2.4).
- **SKYNET 6 Narrowband Satellite System: not proceeding.** SKYNET 5 gets £50m to extend its life; SKYNET 6A is still due to launch in 2027 (2.7). See [[Intelligence/defence-landscape/Programmes/SKYNET|SKYNET]] and [[Intelligence/defence-landscape/Programmes/SKYNET 6A|SKYNET 6A]].
- **Type 23 frigates:** phased retirement up to 2033.
- **Offshore Patrol Vessels:** future plans under review.
- **Defence Industrial Strategy work programmes:** to be reviewed and rescoped as the plan is implemented (3.2).

## Money from outside the MOD

The plan also points to finance routes that matter to founders raising money (section 1.3 to 1.4):

- The **National Wealth Fund** has made its first defence-sector investment.
- The **British Business Bank** is working to improve access to capital for innovative defence and dual-use SMEs, "through the investment of hundreds of millions". No named scheme yet.
- The **[[CRM/organisations/mod/Defence Investors Advisory Group|Defence Investors' Advisory Group]]** advises on making defence more investable, and a **Defence Investment Unit** in the NAD Group is the MOD's centre of expertise for finance and investment.
- Still to come: a **Defence Finance and Investment Strategy**, a **Defence Investment Summit** and a **Defence Investment Roadmap** (no dates given), and a **Multilateral Defence Mechanism**, an international lending institution being set up with allies including Finland and the Netherlands.

## How to use this page

- Match your technology to a line above, then find who spends it: most of it flows through the [[CRM/organisations/mod/NAD Group/NAD Group|NAD Group]], the innovation money through [[CRM/organisations/mod/NAD Group/National Armaments Innovation (NA-Innov)|UKDI in NA-Innov]], and the AI money through the [[CRM/organisations/mod/Defence AI Centre (DAIC)|Defence AI Centre]] and Taskforce RAID.
- Check UKDI's [live funding opportunities](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/live-funding-opportunities) and the [[Intelligence/defence-landscape/Portals/MOD Acquisition Pipeline|MOD Acquisition Pipeline]] to see the money turn into competitions and tenders.
- Watch for the annual delivery update (first due by July 2027): it will show what moved.
