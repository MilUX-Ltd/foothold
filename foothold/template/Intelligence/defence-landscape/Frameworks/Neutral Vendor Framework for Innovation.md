# Neutral Vendor Framework for Innovation

> **Corrected October 2026.** Earlier versions of this page described NVFi as a Crown Commercial Service framework. It is an **MOD** framework, run by **Commercial X**, with **Constellia** as the neutral vendor. The MOD's [SME Action Plan](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mod-small-and-medium-sized-enterprise-sme-action-plan) lists NVFi among the routes Commercial X runs.

## Summary

NVFi uses the **neutral-vendor procurement model** to give public-sector buyers fast access to novel technology. A single neutral vendor sits between the buyer and a panel of pre-accredited technology providers: it receives requirements, runs competitions or direct awards against the panel, contracts on the buyer's behalf, and must stay neutral in choosing providers (it does not supply technology itself).

Current facts from the MOD's routes-to-market page (updated 18 September 2026): **open to new entrants**; R&D across **14 categories**, from AI to the Internet of Things; **contracts up to £50m**; supplier registration is through Constellia. The MOD appointed Constellia as the neutral vendor in September 2024 (trade press).

For a founder, NVFi is a way to be buyable by any public body without winning a framework place of your own: join the Constellia panel in your category.

Related Commercial X routes: [[Intelligence/defence-landscape/Frameworks/Commercial X and Defence Unicorns|Commercial X and Defence Unicorns]].

## Operating Model: the neutral-vendor pattern

A neutral-vendor framework typically works like this:

1. **Lead neutral vendor.** The contracting authority (for NVFi, the MOD) appoints a single lead neutral-vendor organisation to act as the prime contractor across the framework. The neutral vendor is contractually distinct from the end-supplier community: it manages the supply chain rather than competing in it.
2. **Supplier panel.** The neutral vendor maintains a panel of innovation-services suppliers (SMEs, consultancies, research organisations) that have met the framework's conditions for membership.
3. **Buyer ordering.** Public-sector buyers contract with the neutral vendor rather than directly with the end-supplier. The neutral vendor runs the competition or direct award against the panel and contracts the chosen supplier on the buyer's behalf.
4. **Risk transfer.** The neutral vendor takes commercial responsibility for delivery, with the end-supplier as its sub-contractor.
5. **Innovation focus.** Where the framework is specifically branded "for innovation", the scope typically covers R&D services, applied innovation, design-thinking support, and similar advisory services that fit poorly into traditional outcome-based digital frameworks.

### Where this pattern fits alongside other frameworks

Compared with the Government Commercial Agency (GCA, formerly Crown Commercial Service) digital frameworks:

- [[Intelligence/defence-landscape/Frameworks/G-Cloud|G-Cloud]]: buyer contracts directly with the end-supplier (no neutral vendor); cloud services scope.
- [[Intelligence/defence-landscape/Frameworks/Digital Outcomes and Specialists (DOS)|DOS]]: buyer contracts directly with the end-supplier; digital outcomes and specialists scope (including user research).
- **Neutral-vendor frameworks**: buyer contracts with the neutral vendor; end-suppliers sit one tier below. Used where the buyer wants a managed-service overlay or where the supplier base is more fragmented than fits a direct-contract model.

The trade-off: neutral-vendor frameworks add a margin layer for the neutral vendor but reduce buyer-side procurement burden and supply-chain risk.
