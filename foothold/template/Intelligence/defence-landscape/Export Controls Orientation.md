# Export Controls Orientation

This is a screening orientation, not legal advice, and export controls carry criminal liability, so the exit from this page is often "get specialist advice". Its job is to make sure you know when to take that exit. OGELs and control lists change; where this page and gov.uk disagree, gov.uk wins.

## The UK regime in one page

- **What is controlled**: anything on the [UK Strategic Export Control Lists](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-strategic-export-control-lists-the-consolidated-list-of-strategic-military-and-dual-use-items-that-require-export-authorisation), which consolidate the military list and the dual-use list. Goods, software and **technology** (technical data, know-how) are all in scope.
- **Who regulates**: the **Export Control Joint Unit (ECJU)**, which administers the UK's export controls and licensing for military and dual-use items. gov.uk lists it as part of the [[CRM/organisations/government/BIST|Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade (BIST)]], the renamed Department for Business and Trade. Licence applications run through **SPIRE**, the online export licensing system, and ECJU's newer system **LITE**: since Notice to exporters 2026/21 (31 July 2026), F680 applications (approval to promote or supply classified equipment or information overseas) go through LITE rather than SPIRE.
- **Licence types**: SIEL (single shipment, single consignee), OIEL (open individual, repeated exports), and OGELs (open general licences anyone can register to use if they meet the conditions).
- **Current as of October 2026**:
  - A new **Dual-Use OGEL** (open general export licence: export of dual-use items) came into force on 25 June 2026, streamlining dual-use exports to certain low-risk destinations; it requires SPIRE registration and four-year record-keeping.
  - A new **open licence under the Agreement on Defence Export Controls**, for "de minimis" exports, was published on 9 September 2026 alongside an updated GCAP open licence (Notice to exporters 2026/19).
  - The **Control List Classification (CLC) service is paused** from 12 October 2026 (Notice to exporters 2026/22). Requests already submitted are still being processed, more slowly; new requests will not be processed; ECJU will update before the end of the year. You remain responsible for deciding whether your goods, software or technology are controlled.
  - Check the [OGEL collection](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/open-general-export-licences-ogels) and the [notices to exporters](https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/notices-to-exporters) for the live position.

## The trap founders miss: transfer counts as export

You do not need to put anything in a crate to make a licensable export.

- **Emailing controlled technical data** to someone abroad is an export.
- **Giving a non-UK national access** to controlled technology, including an employee or contractor working in the UK, can be a licensable transfer.
- **Cloud storage and remote access** can move controlled data across borders without anyone deciding to.
- **US-origin content contaminates.** Components, software or technical data of US origin can drag **ITAR** or **EAR** obligations into your product, with the US regime's own rules on retransfer. One US-origin subsystem can constrain your whole export position.

## Screening checklist

Work through these before you pitch internationally, demo abroad, publish technical detail, or hire onto controlled work. Any yes means check the control lists properly and consider specialist advice.

1. Does your technology touch typical controlled domains: AI/autonomy for military application, encryption, GNSS/PNT, advanced materials, UxVs, sensing and electronic warfare, propulsion, hypersonics?
2. Is anything you make designed or modified for military use, or sold to a military end user?
3. Do you employ or plan to employ non-UK nationals with access to technical data in scope?
4. Is there US-origin content (hardware, software, or data) anywhere in your stack?
5. Are you about to export, demo, or transfer anything to a destination outside the UK, including electronically?

## Where to get real help

- ECJU's [export controls guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/export-controls-dual-use-items-software-and-technology-goods-for-torture-and-radioactive-sources) on gov.uk. Do not plan on ECJU's control list classification service while it is paused (see above); classify against the control lists yourself, and take specialist advice if you are unsure.
- ECJU training courses (gov.uk lists current dates).
- **EGAD** (Export Group for Aerospace, Defence and Dual-Use), the industry body, publishes practical guidance.
- For ITAR/EAR questions, specialist counsel. This is the one area where an hour of advice early is reliably cheaper than the alternative.
