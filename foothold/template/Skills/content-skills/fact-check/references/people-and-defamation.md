# Statements about people and companies

This is a flag list, not legal advice. It helps the user notice wording that could cause trouble before it goes out. Anything that may already be defamatory, or that names someone in a dispute, should go to a solicitor before publication.

## What defamation is, in brief

Defamation is publishing a statement about an identifiable person or organisation that lowers them in the eyes of reasonable people. In England and Wales the main statute is the Defamation Act 2013: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2013/26/contents

Points worth knowing:

- **Serious harm.** A statement is only defamatory if it has caused, or is likely to cause, serious harm to the reputation of the person. For a body that trades for profit, harm is not serious unless it has caused, or is likely to cause, the body serious financial loss (Defamation Act 2013, section 1(2)).
- **Identifiable is enough.** The person need not be named. "A certain Leeds accountancy firm run by two brothers" may identify them.
- **Repeating counts.** Sharing, quoting or linking someone else's allegation can make you responsible for it. "Reportedly" does not protect you.
- **Main defences** under the 2013 Act include truth (the statement is substantially true, and you can prove it), honest opinion (clearly opinion, based on facts indicated, that an honest person could hold), and publication on a matter of public interest (with a reasonable belief that publishing was in the public interest). Each has conditions; they are not automatic.
- **Different law across the UK.** Scotland has its own law (the Defamation and Malicious Publication (Scotland) Act 2021) and Northern Ireland has its own Act (2022). Note this if the draft is aimed at readers there.
- **Related risks.** A false statement about a business's goods or services made with malice can be malicious falsehood even without reputational harm. Comparative advertising about a competitor is also covered by the CAP Code.

## What to flag

Flag any statement about a real, identifiable person or organisation that says or suggests they:

- acted dishonestly, unlawfully, or broke rules or regulations;
- are incompetent, negligent or unsafe;
- treat customers, staff or suppliers badly;
- failed, went bust, or are in financial trouble (check Companies House before saying anything about status);
- were involved in a case, complaint or dispute (check the outcome, not just the allegation).

Also flag "named and shamed" examples, negative reviews of a competitor, and case studies that describe a client's past problems without their agreement.

## Suggesting neutral wording

- **State what can be proved and attributed.** "The ASA upheld a complaint against X in March 2024 about a pricing claim" (with a link to the ruling) instead of "X misleads customers".
- **Separate fact from opinion,** and show the facts the opinion rests on.
- **Remove the name** when the point does not need it. "Many firms still..." often does the job.
- **Use the official outcome,** not the original allegation.
- **Ask permission** for client stories, and let the client approve the wording.

## How to report it

In the claims table, give these the verdict that fits the evidence and add a "Defamation flag" note. In must-fix, say in one or two plain sentences what the risk is, suggest the neutral wording, and say: "This is a flag, not legal advice. If you want to keep the original wording, take advice from a solicitor first."
