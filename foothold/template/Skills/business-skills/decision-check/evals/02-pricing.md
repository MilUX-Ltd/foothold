# Eval 02: Raising prices

Synthetic case. All names and figures are invented.

## Set-up

Gareth owns a bike repair and servicing shop in Cardiff with three staff. He has not raised his standard service price (£65) for three years. Parts and wages have gone up. Annual turnover is about £84,000 and he is not VAT-registered. He is thinking of a rise to £79.

## Opening prompt

> Should I put my service price up to £79? I'm worried I'll lose customers.

## The skill should

1. Frame the decision in one sentence and confirm Gareth decides.
2. Size it as Standard: reversible (prices can come down) but with real cost if customers leave, and reputational risk if he reverses.
3. Offer options including **do nothing**, **wait** (for example until after the spring rush, with what that would show), a smaller rise, a phased rise, or a rise for new customers only.
4. Agree weighted criteria such as margin, customer retention, cash, simplicity.
5. Separate known (current costs, current price, number of services last year) from assumed ("customers will leave" [to verify]; how many, and how to test cheaply, for example checking competitors' published prices or trialling the rise on one service).
6. Check **anchoring**: is £79 or the old £65 setting the frame instead of his costs and local prices? Check **status quo bias**, given no rise for three years. Explain each with its origin.
7. Raise the UK check that plainly applies: **VAT registration**. A rise could push turnover past the VAT registration threshold (£90,000 from April 2024 [to verify current figure]); if so, he would have to register and add VAT, which matters because his customers are mostly consumers who cannot reclaim it. Link the gov.uk page and suggest an accountant.
8. Run a pre-mortem crediting Gary Klein.
9. Recommend, with what would change the answer (for example a drop in bookings above an agreed level within two months) and a review date.
10. Offer the decision note.

## Pass

- Spots the VAT threshold interaction without being prompted, as a question to check, with the link and [to verify] on the figure.
- Includes "do nothing" and "wait".
- Explains anchoring and status quo bias in plain words with their origins.
- Sets a review date and a measurable warning sign.

## Fail

- States the VAT threshold as current fact without a check marker, or tells Gareth he must or need not register.
- Invents competitor prices or market data.
- Skips the cash and VAT effect entirely.
