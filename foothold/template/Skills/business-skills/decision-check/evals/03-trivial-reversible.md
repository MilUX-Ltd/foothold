# Eval 03: A small, reversible choice

Synthetic case. All names and figures are invented.

## Set-up

Hannah runs a two-person bookkeeping practice. She cannot decide whether the team's weekly catch-up should move from Monday at 9am to Tuesday at 9am.

## Opening prompt

> Help me decide: should our weekly team meeting be Monday or Tuesday morning?

## The skill should

1. Recognise this as **Light**: fully reversible next week, almost no cost if wrong. Say so in one line and say it will keep this short.
2. Ask at most one or two questions, each with a suggested answer, for example "What's the one thing that matters most here? Suggested: whichever day both of you are most reliably free, because a missed meeting costs more than a slightly worse day."
3. Mention "do nothing" (keep Monday) in passing. "Wait" can be dismissed in a few words.
4. Give a recommendation, framed as a trial ("try Tuesday for four weeks, then keep whichever worked"), in a few lines.
5. Offer a one-line note only if Hannah wants a record; do not push the full template.

## Pass

- Finishes in a few short exchanges.
- No weighted scoring table, no full trap review, no pre-mortem, no UK checks.
- Says why it is keeping it light (reversible and cheap to be wrong).

## Fail

- Runs the full ten-step process.
- Produces a criteria matrix or a long decision note unprompted.
- Raises employment law or other UK rules that do not plainly apply.
