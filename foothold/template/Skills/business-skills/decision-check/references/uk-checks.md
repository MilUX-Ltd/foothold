# UK checks

Read this at Step 8 of `SKILL.md`. Raise only the checks that plainly bear on the decision. Each is a question for the user to check, with the official page to start from. None of this is legal, tax or financial advice, and rules and thresholds change: any figure here is marked "[to verify]" and must be confirmed on the linked page on the day.

Say when a professional is needed. As a rule of thumb: an accountant or tax adviser for VAT, IR35 and tax timing; a solicitor or HR adviser for dismissals, disputes and contracts; the ICO's own guidance, and a data protection specialist if the data is sensitive or large in volume.

## Tax and VAT

Bears on: pricing changes, a big new contract, growth plans, buying equipment.

- **VAT registration.** "Will this push your VAT-taxable turnover over the registration threshold in any rolling 12-month period, or within the next 30 days on its own?" The threshold was £90,000 from 1 April 2024 [to verify current figure]. Crossing it means registering and charging VAT, which changes prices for customers who cannot reclaim VAT. https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat
- **Prices and VAT.** "If you are VAT-registered, are your new prices shown inclusive of VAT to consumers, with the total price, including taxes and mandatory fees, shown up front?" The VAT page explains how to work out the price: https://www.gov.uk/charge-reclaim-record-vat. The duty to show the total price comes from the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024; the CMA's price transparency guidance (CMA209) explains it: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/price-transparency-cma209
- **Timing against tax deadlines.** "Does the spending fall before or after your year end, and does that matter for your tax?" Ask the accountant. Corporation Tax: https://www.gov.uk/corporation-tax. Self Assessment deadlines: https://www.gov.uk/self-assessment-tax-returns/deadlines

## Contractors and IR35

Bears on: taking on a contractor or freelancer instead of an employee, or a person working through their own limited company.

**What IR35 is.** The "off-payroll working rules", known as IR35 after the 1999 Inland Revenue press release that announced them, decide whether someone working through their own company would be an employee if engaged directly. If so, income tax and National Insurance are due as if employed. Who decides the status depends on the client's size: medium and large clients must decide and issue a status determination; for small clients outside the public sector, the contractor's own company decides. A client company is small if it meets at least two of the Companies Act 2006 small-company tests (turnover, balance sheet total, 50 employees or fewer); the money tests rose for financial years beginning on or after 6 April 2025, so check HMRC's current figures.

- "Is your business small for these rules, and so who is responsible for the status decision?"
- "Would this person, in practice, be told how and when to work, be unable to send a substitute, and work only for you?" Those point towards employment.
- HMRC guidance: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/understanding-off-payroll-working-ir35
- HMRC's Check Employment Status for Tax tool: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/check-employment-status-for-tax
- Employment status more widely: https://www.gov.uk/employment-status

## Hiring

Bears on: whether and whom to hire, part-time against full-time, first employee.

- "Is this your first employee?" If so, there are set-up duties: registering as an employer, employer's liability insurance, a written statement of terms, and more. https://www.gov.uk/get-ready-to-employ-someone
- "Have you checked right to work, before the start date?" https://www.gov.uk/check-job-applicant-right-to-work
- "Have you allowed for automatic enrolment into a workplace pension in the cost?" https://www.gov.uk/workplace-pensions-employers
- "Have you priced in the National Minimum Wage or National Living Wage for this age band?" https://www.gov.uk/national-minimum-wage-rates
- ACAS guidance on hiring: https://www.acas.org.uk/hiring-someone
- Employment law is changing under the Employment Rights Act 2025 reforms, phased in from 2026 [to verify what is in force on the day]. Ask whether any change affects probation, contracts or rights from day one.

## Letting someone go

Bears on: dismissal, redundancy, ending a contract, restructuring.

This is the area where a professional is most often needed before acting. Say so.

- "Has the person worked for you long enough to have protection against unfair dismissal?" The qualifying period is two years, falling to six months for dismissals with an effective date of termination on or after 1 January 2027 under the Employment Rights Act 2025 [to verify on the day].
- "Is this about conduct, capability or redundancy?" Each has its own fair process.
- "Have you followed, or will you follow, the ACAS Code of Practice on disciplinary and grievance procedures?" A tribunal can adjust awards if it is not followed. https://www.acas.org.uk/acas-code-of-practice-on-disciplinary-and-grievance-procedures
- ACAS on dismissals: https://www.acas.org.uk/dismissals
- ACAS on redundancy: https://www.acas.org.uk/redundancy
- ACAS helpline and early conciliation are free: https://www.acas.org.uk/contact

## Personal data and UK GDPR

Bears on: a new system, a CRM, marketing lists, using AI tools on customer or staff data, sharing data with a supplier.

- "What personal data does this involve, and what is your lawful basis for using it?" https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations/
- "Does the supplier process personal data for you, and do you have a written contract with them that covers it?"
- "Is any data special category (health, ethnicity, religion and so on) or about children?" If so, take more care and consider a data protection impact assessment.
- "Have you paid the ICO data protection fee, if you need to?" https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/data-protection-fee/
- "Will data leave the UK?" International transfers have extra rules.

## Cash flow

Bears on: almost any decision that spends money or changes when it comes in.

- "Month by month for the next six months, can you pay wages, suppliers, VAT and tax when they fall due, with this decision taken?" Sketch the timing even roughly.
- "What is the worst realistic case for cash, and could you survive it?"
- "Are any HMRC payments due soon that this would squeeze?" PAYE: https://www.gov.uk/pay-paye-tax. VAT: https://www.gov.uk/pay-vat
- If cash is tight with HMRC, they offer Time to Pay arrangements: https://www.gov.uk/difficulties-paying-hmrc
- Free business support: https://www.gov.uk/get-business-support (the Business Support Service in England; Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have their own services, listed on the same page)
