# Estimate Market Size (TAM, SAM, SOM)

Forked from [phuryn/pm-skills](https://github.com/phuryn/pm-skills) v2.0.0 (`pm-market-research/market-sizing`, MIT, copyright Pawel Huryn). MIT licence text retained in `LICENSE` alongside this file. **Modified by MilUX:** adapted for defence-sector founders and reframed in plain language for public release; the original method and attribution are retained. Modifications copyright 2026 MilUX Ltd, released under the same MIT licence.

## What this is

You size the opportunity, for an investment case, a bid case, or a market-entry decision.

## The defence move

The TAM, SAM, SOM method holds; the inputs change. Size against **programmes and budgets**, not a consumer total-addressable-market figure:

- **TAM**, the total relevant spend: the programme, category or line-of-development budget, read from the Defence Equipment Plan, published MOD spend and category data.
- **SAM**, the portion realistically serviceable, gated by clearances, framework and contract access (for example DSP and specific framework agreements), accreditation, and product capability.
- **SOM**, the portion obtainable in one to three years, gated by incumbents, primes, the route to contract, and the **valley of death** between trial and scaled procurement.

## Method

1. **Define the market:** the problem space, the segment, the geography and the constraints.
2. **Top-down:** start from the total programme or category spend and narrow to the relevant slice. Cite sources.
3. **Bottom-up:** build from units (customers by contract value by frequency) to cross-validate.
4. **Scope SAM:** the slice the offering can serve given clearances, frameworks, accreditation and capability.
5. **Estimate SOM:** the achievable share given the competitive and procurement reality.
6. **Project growth:** how each may move over two to three years.
7. **Surface assumptions:** number them, rate confidence, and say how to validate the most uncertain.

## Output

A market definition; TAM, SAM and SOM each with method, sources and reasoning; a summary table (current and 2 to 3 year projection); the growth drivers; and the numbered key assumptions with confidence levels and validation steps.

## Notes

- Always give both top-down and bottom-up; triangulate.
- Use web research for current MOD and programme data; cite sources, never assert unsupported numbers.
- Label estimates against data, and flag wide confidence intervals.
- A defence opportunity can have a large TAM and a tiny SOM because of the valley of death; say so plainly.
- Ground the programme and budget picture in current published MOD data and the Defence Industrial Strategy.
- Pairs with `market-segments`, `beachhead-segment`, `strategy-red-team`.
