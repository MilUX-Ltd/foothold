## What this is, and how it relates to value-proposition

`value-proposition` works out *what* the value is, one segment at a time, as how well the offering fits what the customer selects on. This skill turns that into the *copy*: statements for marketing, sales and onboarding. Run them in that order.

## Method

From the value proposition(s) for **$ARGUMENTS**, write statements that each:

- Address one specific segment or use case.
- Lead with the primary benefit and the outcome delivered.
- Name the capability that makes it possible.
- Use clear, compelling language in the audience's terms.

Produce a statement per segment and per context (marketing, sales or bid, onboarding). Keep the customer and the user distinct; the statement that persuades a commercial officer is not the one that reassures an operator.

## Worked example (defence)

- **For a capability lead:** "[Offering] gives your team a shared operating picture that holds without comms, accreditable on your existing devices, so an exercise runs on one common picture instead of paper and voice, at a fraction of the through-life cost of the prime alternative."
- **For an operator:** "[Offering] works the way you do on the ground: offline-first, low training burden, one tap to share what you see."
