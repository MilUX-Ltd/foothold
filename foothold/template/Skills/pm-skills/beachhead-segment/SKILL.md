# Beachhead Segment

Forked from [phuryn/pm-skills](https://github.com/phuryn/pm-skills) v2.0.0 (`pm-go-to-market/beachhead-segment`, MIT, copyright Pawel Huryn). Based on Geoffrey Moore's beachhead strategy in *Crossing the Chasm*. MIT licence text retained in `LICENSE` alongside this file. **Modified by MilUX:** adapted for defence-sector founders and reframed in plain language for public release; the original method and attribution are retained. Modifications copyright 2026 MilUX Ltd, released under the same MIT licence.

## What this is

You choose the beachhead. It follows segmentation and the ICP, and feeds the value proposition and the capture plan.

## The four criteria, framed for defence

1. **Acute, unmet need.** Does this segment carry a real capability gap or operational pain, getting worse, with fragile or expensive workarounds? In defence the burning pain is usually an operational shortfall or a mandated requirement, not a productivity annoyance.

2. **Ability to contract.** Is there budget and a route to put it under contract? A named programme or budget line, a clear value case, a sponsor with influence over the decision, and a framework or procurement route you can actually use. Willingness to pay alone is not enough if there is no route to contract. **Qualify the funding explicitly: interest is not budget authority.** A warm meeting, a trial, or a hub's attention is not a funded route. Establish whether the effort is actually funded, at what order of magnitude, and on what timeframe; and distinguish a hub (exposure) and an accelerator (prototype funding, for example DIANA) from a gatekeeper who controls the budget and the requirement.

3. **Winnability.** Can you realistically win and hold this segment? Read the incumbent, the primes, the differentiation, and the distribution or access advantage. Be honest about the **valley of death**: a segment where you can win a trial but cannot cross to scaled procurement is not yet winnable.

4. **Referral into adjacent segments.** Will winning here open the next one? Defence is a small, networked community; a reference customer, a successful trial, or an adjacent programme that watches this one is the expansion path.

## Method

1. **List candidate segments** from the segmentation work: purpose-named, not firmographic buckets.
2. **Validate the need** in each with evidence (interviews, requirements, published programmes), and quantify the impact of the gap.
3. **Assess the route to contract**: budget, sponsor, framework, typical deal shape and timeline.
4. **Evaluate winnability**: incumbent, primes, differentiation, and the valley-of-death risk.
5. **Map referral pathways**: adjacent programmes, the defence community, reference potential.
6. **Choose the beachhead**: highest combined score, most achievable now, shortest honest path to a contract and a reference, best springboard to the next segment.

## Output

Top three to five segments scored against the four criteria; the recommended beachhead with the evidence behind it; a capture plan for it; and the expansion path to adjacent segments once it is held.

## Notes

- Start specific. A narrow, winnable beachhead beats a vague large market.
- Do not plan to leave the beachhead until it is genuinely held and referenceable.
- Pairs with `ideal-customer-profile`, `market-sizing`, `value-proposition`, and `strategy-red-team` (stress-test the choice).
