# Porter's Five Forces

## What this is

A structural read of an industry's attractiveness and the forces that set profitability. One of a market-scan set. The defence reframing is where the value is: the forces behave differently in a market with one dominant buyer and a tier of primes.

## The five forces, read for defence

1. **Competitive rivalry**, primes and specialist SMEs; slow, programme-driven demand; differentiation through capability and accreditation rather than price.
2. **Supplier power**, sub-tier suppliers, sovereign-capability constraints, single-source components, export-controlled inputs.
3. **Buyer power**, high. The MOD is a near-monopsony customer with strong bargaining power, structured competitions, and the route to contract on its terms. Selling through a prime shifts, but does not remove, this.
4. **Threat of substitutes**, alternative capabilities, in-house build, doing nothing, or a different operational concept that removes the need.
5. **Threat of new entrants**, bounded by high barriers: clearances, accreditation (CE Plus, JOSCAR), framework access, incumbent relationships, and the capital and time to cross the valley of death.

## Method

Rate each force (high, medium, low) with its current state and trend; judge overall attractiveness; pick the two or three forces most critical to the strategy; develop responses (reduce the high-power forces, exploit the weak ones); identify positioning opportunities. Render the result.

## Notes

- The buyer-power and new-entrant forces in defence are structural: the near-monopsony MOD, the prime tier, and accreditation and valley-of-death barriers.
- No market is attractive or unattractive in the abstract; position decides it.
- Pairs with `swot-analysis`, `pestle-analysis`, `ansoff-matrix`, `competitor-analysis`.
