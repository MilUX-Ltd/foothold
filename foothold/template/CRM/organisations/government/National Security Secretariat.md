# National Security Secretariat (NSS)

The Cabinet Office secretariat that **coordinates security and intelligence issues of strategic importance across government** and supports the **National Security Adviser (NSA)** and the **National Security Council (NSC)**. Sits inside the wider National Security and Intelligence directorate at the Cabinet Office.

GOV.UK: [National Security Council](https://www.gov.uk/government/groups/national-security-council); [National Security and Intelligence](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/national-security). Parliamentary research: [The UK National Security Council (Commons Library briefing, PDF)](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/CBP-7456/CBP-7456.pdf).

## Role in the landscape

The NSS supports the apex of the UK's national-security machinery: the NSC chaired by the Prime Minister and the National Security Adviser who advises the PM and Cabinet. The NSC was established by Prime Minister David Cameron on **12 May 2010** and formalised national-security decision-making that had previously sat in informal official-level groups.

### What the NSC and NSA do

- Convene Cabinet ministers (including Foreign Secretary, Home Secretary, Defence Secretary, Chancellor, plus senior intelligence chiefs) to discuss national security strategy, foreign policy, defence, trade, international relations, development, resilience and resource security.
- Until 2024 the NSA acted as Secretary to the NSC and head of the NSS. The NSA model has since evolved.

### Directorate structure (historical and current)

At a 2014 reference point the NSS comprised five directorates: Foreign & Defence Policy; Civil Contingencies Secretariat; Security & Intelligence; Office of Cyber Security and Information Assurance; and UK Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT UK). The structure has since evolved: Civil Contingencies functions have moved into the [[CRM/organisations/government/Resilience Directorate|Resilience Directorate]] and COBR ecosystem; cyber-security functions sit primarily with [[CRM/organisations/government/NCSC|NCSC]]; the Security & Intelligence and Foreign & Defence Policy directorates remain.

### Why it matters

The NSS is the cross-government conductor for security strategy. Documents like the UK National Security Strategy are NSS-shepherded through NSC clearance before publication.
