# National Security Online Information Team (NSOIT)

The UK government's lead on online mis- and disinformation that touches national security. Founded **2019** as the **Counter Disinformation Unit (CDU)**; rebranded **NSOIT in October 2023** with a revised remit, inside the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology (DSIT). DSIT is being dissolved, so NSOIT's parent is under review following the July 2026 machinery of government changes.

GOV.UK: [NSOIT privacy notice](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-security-online-information-team-privacy-notice/national-security-online-information-team-privacy-notice). Parliamentary record: [NSOIT written question 2024](https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2024-03-11/17886/).

## Role in the landscape

NSOIT's stated remit is to tackle the greatest national security risks facing the UK from mis- and disinformation. Three named priority areas:

- **Threats posed by foreign states.**
- **Risks to elections.**
- **AI and deepfakes.**

The team works with **publicly available information** (per gov.uk privacy notice) and feeds aggregated analysis into the wider government understanding of the online information environment. The 2023 rebrand was a deliberate scope-narrowing exercise: from the broader "counter-disinformation" framing to a national-security-anchored one.

### Where it sits

Inside DSIT from 2023. DSIT is being dissolved, and the July 2026 changes give mis- and disinformation levers to the expanded Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport, but gov.uk does not yet name NSOIT's new home. Treat its parent as under review following the July 2026 machinery of government changes. Source: gov.uk [Machinery of government changes fact sheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/machinery-of-government-changes-fact-sheet).

### Controversy

The unit has faced criticism on civil-liberties grounds, particularly around scope-creep into political speech rather than purely foreign-state activity. The 2023 rebrand and remit narrowing was in part a response. Founders engaging in information-integrity work should be aware of the policy debate around the unit's scope.
