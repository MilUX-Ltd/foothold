# Resilience Directorate

The Cabinet Office directorate established in **September 2022** to take a strategic, cross-system view of UK national resilience. Created when the Cabinet Office reorganised its existing resilience functions to **separate preparedness-and-risk-reduction from crisis-response**: the Resilience Directorate owns the former; the Cabinet Office Briefing Rooms (COBR) Unit owns the latter.

Key publications: [The UK Government Resilience Framework (Dec 2022)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-uk-government-resilience-framework/the-uk-government-resilience-framework-html); [2023 Implementation Update](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-resilience-framework-2023-implementation-update/the-uk-government-resilience-framework-2023-implementation-update-html); [UK Government Resilience Action Plan](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/uk-government-resilience-action-plan/uk-government-resilience-action-plan-html).

## Role in the landscape

The Resilience Directorate exists to drive implementation of the UK Government Resilience Framework and to develop the ongoing resilience programme across government. It works alongside the UK Government's crisis-management infrastructure (the COBR ecosystem) rather than inside it, holding the longer-arc planning and capability-building remit.

### The UK Government Resilience Framework

Published December 2022. Sets out commitments across six themes:

- **Risk** (assessing and communicating threat).
- **Responsibilities and Accountability** (who owns what).
- **Partnerships** (the "whole of society" framing).
- **Communities** (local resilience).
- **Skills** (the UK Resilience Academy).
- **Investment** (funding model).

Named deliverables from the framework: annual statement to Parliament on civil contingency risk and resilience; UK Resilience Academy; Head of Resilience appointment; the UK Government Resilience Action Plan.

### "Whole of society" framing

The framework explicitly frames resilience as a multi-actor endeavour. Government leads, but relies on industry, communities, and individuals. This is the policy frame that any UK resilience-tech engagement needs to fit inside.

### Public-facing surface

The [GOV Prepare](https://prepare.campaign.gov.uk/) site is the public-facing complement to the framework, aimed at individuals and communities.
