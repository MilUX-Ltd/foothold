# UK National Authority for Counter-Eavesdropping (UK NACE)

The UK's **National Technical Authority for Technical Security** — the practice of protecting sensitive information and technology from close-access acquisition by hostile threat actors, and from technical manipulation. One of three UK National Technical Authorities, alongside [[CRM/organisations/government/NCSC|NCSC]] (cyber) and [[CRM/organisations/government/National Protective Security Authority|NPSA]] (physical and personnel).

FCDO Services: [UK NACE homepage](https://www.fcdoservices.gov.uk/uk-nace/). GOV.UK: [UK National Authority for Counter-Eavesdropping](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/uk-national-authority-for-counter-eavesdropping). NCSC: [About UK NACE](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/organisation/uk-national-authority-for-counter-eavesdropping-uk-nace/about-uk-national-authority-for-counter-eavesdropping-uk-nace).

## Role in the landscape

UK NACE protects national security by providing **Technical Security expertise** to government and Critical National Infrastructure (CNI). Specialism: countering technical espionage (eavesdropping, electromagnetic emanations attack, technical manipulation of sensitive equipment).

### Named functions

- Develop UK government **technical security standards**.
- Provide **training and mentoring** to national and international partners (e.g. the TEMPEST Fundamentals Course).
- Carry out **research and development** of new threats and countermeasure technologies.

### Historical lineage

Evolved from the Diplomatic Wireless Service at Bletchley Park in the 1940s. Established in 1945 after British embassies in the USSR were found to have been subject to technical espionage. From 1960 to 1969 sat inside MI5; transferred back to the Foreign Office (now FCDO) in 1969 and has remained there since. Now part of FCDO Services, the trading-fund arm of FCDO.
