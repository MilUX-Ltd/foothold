# Military Aviation Authority (MAA)

## Summary

The Military Aviation Authority (MAA) is the single, independent regulator responsible for the **regulation, assurance and enforcement of air safety** across UK Defence. Sits within the [[CRM/organisations/mod/Defence Safety Authority (DSA)|Defence Safety Authority (DSA)]] alongside the other defence safety regulators (nuclear, maritime, land, ordnance, explosives, fire).

The MAA was **established in 2010** in response to the recommendations of Mr Justice Haddon-Cave KC's **Nimrod Review** (2009), which called for a radical overhaul of military airworthiness regulation following the loss of XV230 over Afghanistan in 2006. Located at **MOD Abbey Wood (North), Bristol** and led by a **2-star Director (D MAA)**.

GOV.UK: [Military Aviation Authority](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/military-aviation-authority); [About us](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/military-aviation-authority/about). Policy: [MAA01: Military Aviation Authority (PDF, Issue 11)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/662b5ef6ae7fb5d93ebf9306/MAA01_Issue_11.pdf).

## What the MAA does

As the single regulatory authority for air safety across the Defence Air Environment (DAE), the MAA has full oversight of all defence aviation activity. The role is to ensure the safe design and use of military air systems.

### Three groups

- **Regulations and Certification (Reg & Cert).** Delivers the regulations that implement MAA policy across flying, air traffic management (ATM), design, and continuing airworthiness. Also performs technical certification of new air systems.
- **Operating and Airworthiness Group (OAG).** Regulates the operation of all air systems registered as UK military aircraft and all activity within the UK Defence Air Environment. Provides the main delivery arm of the MAA's assurance activity.
- **Air Safety Group.** Manages and analyses safety information, learning, and the Defence Air Safety Information Management System (ASIMS).

## MAA01: the underpinning policy

[MAA01 (Issue 11)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/662b5ef6ae7fb5d93ebf9306/MAA01_Issue_11.pdf) is the MAA's foundational publication, setting out the MAA's policy and authority. It is the equivalent of a regulator's enabling charter and binds the entire Defence Air Environment.

## International recognition

The MAA is mutually recognised by other allied military aviation regulators, including Australia's Defence Aviation Safety Authority. See [DASA Australia: Aviation Authority Recognition Certificate, UK MAA (PDF)](https://dasa.defence.gov.au/sites/default/files/022%20-%20Aviation%20Authority%20Recognition%20Certificate%20-%20United%20Kingdom%20MAA%20(MAA-UK)%20(Public).pdf).

## Place in the wider UK aviation regulatory system

The UK Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) regulates civil aviation; the MAA regulates military aviation. The two co-exist as named stakeholders in the UK State Safety Programme. See [CAA: The UK aviation system](https://www.caa.co.uk/safety-initiatives/how-we-regulate/state-safety-programme/safety-policy-objectives-and-resources/the-uk-aviation-system/) and [CAA: State safety programme stakeholders](https://www.caa.co.uk/safety-initiatives/how-we-regulate/state-safety-programme/safety-policy-objectives-and-resources/state-safety-programme-stakeholders/).

The MAA's scope is the Defence Air Environment, including all UK military aircraft, military Air Traffic Management, design and continuing airworthiness, and any civil operators acting on Defence business under MAA regulation.
