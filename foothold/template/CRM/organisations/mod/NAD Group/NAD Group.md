# National Armaments Director Group (NAD Group)

## Overview

The National Armaments Director Group (NAD Group) is a senior-level organisation within UK Defence responsible for delivering warfighting capability. Established March 2025; restructured 2 April 2026 with new identity, branding, and consolidated leadership under nine Director-General-led areas plus the Defence Infrastructure Organisation. NAD Group oversees the acquisition, integration, fielding, and in-service support of military capability across multiple value streams and mission threads.

NAD Group is led by the **National Armaments Director (Rupert Pearce)**.

**GOV.UK:** [National Armaments Director (NAD) Group](https://www.gov.uk/government/groups/national-armaments-director-nad-group)

## NAD Group Structure (post-2 April 2026)

Nine Director-General-led areas, plus the Defence Infrastructure Organisation (name retained). Brand and naming consolidation announced 2 April 2026 by the National Armaments Director.

NAD Group's areas are grouped functionally across four bands: **Design** (NA-P&P), **Innovation** (NA-Innov), **Delivery** (NA-M, NA-L&S, DIO, NA-D&D), and **Enable** (NA-Intl, NA-C&I, NA-C, Finance).

### Executive agency

- [[CRM/organisations/mod/NAD Group/National Armaments Delivery Executive\|National Armaments Delivery Executive]] — new executive agency holding the freedoms and delegations transferred from the retired DE&S. All former DE&S staff now belong to this agency, working day-to-day to their respective DG.

### Retired brand

- **Defence Equipment & Support (DE&S)** — brand retired 2 April 2026. Core-delivery functions absorbed into NA-M; freedoms and delegations transferred to the National Armaments Delivery Executive. The DE&S CEO role is retired. Staff Terms and Conditions unchanged.

## Strategic context

NAD Group operates in a strategic environment defined by accelerating technological change, contested supply chains, and adversaries that adapt faster than traditional acquisition cycles. The structural challenge it sets out to solve: fragmented delivery, excessive work in progress, long and variable lead times, weak feedback loops, and governance optimised for compliance rather than operational effect.
