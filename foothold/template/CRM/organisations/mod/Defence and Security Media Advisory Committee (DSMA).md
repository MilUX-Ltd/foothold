# Defence and Security Media Advisory Committee (DSMA)

## Summary

A British government media-liaison advisory body operating a **voluntary code** between UK government departments with national-security responsibility and the media. Manages the **DSMA-Notice system** (historically known as "D-Notices"). The Committee is composed of senior civil servants and senior editors from across UK media; the notices it issues have **no formal legal authority** and the final publication decision rests with the editor or publisher.

The DSMA was established in its current form in **2015** following the 2015 review of the D-Notice system, which recommended renaming the body to better reflect the longstanding inclusion of intelligence agencies (not just defence) in the system's scope.

GOV.UK: [Defence and Security Media Advisory Committee](https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/defence-and-security-media-advisory-committee). Committee site: [dsma.uk](https://www.dsma.uk/); [About the system](https://www.dsma.uk/about/); [How the system works](https://www.dsma.uk/how-the-system-works/); [The Committee](https://www.dsma.uk/the-committee/).

## DSMA-Notice history

| Period | Name | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1912 to 1993 | **D-Notice** (Defence Notice) | Original wartime-era body |
| 1993 to mid-2010s | **DA-Notice** (Defence Advisory Notice) | Renamed |
| 2015 to present | **DSMA-Notice** | Current name; broader to include security and intelligence |

The "D-Notice" label persists informally in the media despite the formal renaming.

## Purpose

The stated aim of the DSMA-Notice system is to prevent inadvertent public disclosure of information that would:

- Compromise UK military and intelligence operations and methods.
- Put at risk the safety of those involved in such operations.
- Lead to attacks that would damage critical national infrastructure.
- Endanger lives.

## How the system works

The system is **voluntary**. There are no formal censorship powers. When a media organisation is considering publishing material that may touch DSMA-Notice areas, the editor can consult the DSMA Secretary; the Committee may then issue advisory guidance, but the final decision to publish or broadcast rests with the editor or publisher.

The DSMA-Notice system relies entirely on the editor-side discipline and the trust relationship between government and media.

## Committee composition

The DSMA Committee is independent and advisory. Senior representation comes from:

- Senior civil servants (MOD, FCDO, Home Office, Cabinet Office and the intelligence community).
- Editors and senior figures from national and regional newspapers, periodicals, news agencies, television, radio, and digital publishers.

## Why the DSMA matters for defence-sector founders

The DSMA-Notice system is the operating layer between UK defence/intelligence sensitivity and the open public information environment. Any work that ends up in public output (LinkedIn, articles, podcasts) touching named operations, intelligence methods, infrastructure, or recently-departed personnel could intersect with DSMA-Notice areas. The system is voluntary and operates by trust; mid-career reservists and ex-Service authors are typically aware of it through Service-side education.

Note: the Chatham House Rule deals with a different, also-voluntary disclosure-control regime that applies at the event level. CHR controls speaker identity at events; DSMA-Notice controls subject-matter publication.
