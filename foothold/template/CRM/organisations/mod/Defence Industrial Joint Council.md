# Defence Industrial Joint Council (DIJC)

The MOD's main strategic mechanism for defence-sector engagement under the **Defence Industrial Strategy 2025**. Replaces the former **Defence Suppliers Forum** with a deliberately wider membership: SMEs, investors, academia, technologists, trade unions, and trade associations alongside the primes. Co-chaired by a Defence minister and an industry chief executive. The Defence Investment Plan (30 June 2026) describes the DIJC as the body dedicated to delivering the DIS at pace with industry, academia, primes, SMEs, investors and trade unions.

GOV.UK: [Defence Industrial Joint Council](https://www.gov.uk/government/groups/defence-industrial-joint-council); [First meeting news, 23 June 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/first-meeting-of-defence-industry-body-to-forge-new-partnership-and-industry-mobilisation).

## Role in the landscape

DIJC acts as the **change and delivery mechanism for the Defence Industrial Strategy (DIS)**. It has explicit powers to initiate collaborative activity through its sub-structures. The aim is to improve economic efficiency by channelling information between market participants and government, overcoming coordination issues, shaping public-private investment strategies, and involving all relevant stakeholders in the collective endeavour of national defence.

### Membership

Membership is deliberately broad — designed to bring perspectives the Defence Suppliers Forum systematically missed:

- Defence suppliers of all sizes (including SMEs).
- Investors.
- Academia.
- Technologists.
- Trade associations.
- Trade unions (the first time these have been seated alongside SMEs and investors at a senior defence-industry coordination forum).

### Communications Forum

The **DIJC Communications Forum** is the strategic engagement platform: senior representatives from across government, industry, and other stakeholders meeting on a structured cadence to coordinate.

### Why it matters for SMEs

DIJC is the closest thing the post-2025 settlement has to an official seat-at-the-table for SMEs in defence-industrial policy. The mechanism is filtered through trade associations and selected SMEs rather than open membership, but the channel exists.
