# Defence Safety Authority (DSA)

## Summary

The Defence Safety Authority is the MOD's independent **Regulator** and **Investigator** for **Health, Safety and Environmental Protection (HS&EP)** in Defence. Established by a **Charter signed by the Secretary of State** that empowers the DSA as an independent body within the MOD. **Formed on 1 April 2015** following Lord Levene's 2011 review of Defence Reform, which recommended bringing all of Defence's safety regulators under one independent organisation.

Standards: [Acquisition Safety and Environmental Management System (ASEMS)](https://www.asems.mod.uk/printpdf/ExtReferences).

## What the DSA does

The DSA has three named roles:

1. **Regulate safety across Defence** across six domains: aviation, nuclear, maritime, land, ordnance and explosives, fire.
2. **Investigate Defence accidents.**
3. **Act as the Defence Authority for safety** (including health and environmental protection).

### The six regulators inside the DSA

The DSA contains the named regulators for each safety domain inside Defence:

| Regulator | Domain |
|---|---|
| [[CRM/organisations/mod/Military Aviation Authority (MAA)\|Military Aviation Authority (MAA)]] | Military aviation safety |
| Defence Nuclear Safety Regulator (DNSR) | Defence nuclear safety |
| Defence Maritime Regulator (DMR) | Defence maritime safety |
| Defence Land Safety Regulator (DLSR) | Defence land safety (including training accidents) |
| Defence Ordnance, Munitions and Explosives Safety Regulator (DOSR) | Ordnance, munitions, and explosives safety |
| Defence Fire Safety Regulator (DFSR) | Fire safety |

## Independence

The DSA Charter sets out the independence model:

- **Operational independence** from MOD activity holders.
- **Director General DSA is personally appointed by the Secretary of State** and has the right of **direct access to the Secretary of State** to raise safety concerns that are not being satisfactorily dealt with through normal processes.
- **Organisational separation** between those carrying out Defence activities and those providing regulation.
- Not subject to undue financial, political, or operational pressures.

## Notable historical context

The DSA's creation was driven by the **Lord Levene 2011 report** on Defence Reform, which followed the **Haddon-Cave Nimrod Review** (2009) for aviation. The cross-domain pattern that emerged was: bring the named safety regulators under one independent organisation, with charter-backed independence and a personal Secretary of State link for the DG.

The Commons Defence Committee report *Beyond endurance? Military exercises and the duty of care* references the DSA structure in the context of training-accident investigation.
